\section{Caching and Verification}
Exact caching uses request identity as its reuse condition, in the spirit of classic lexical retrieval that scores documents by weighted term overlap \cite{salton1988term}. Semantic caching instead retrieves neighbors in an embedding space and adds a decision rule. Transformer language models \cite{vaswani2017attention,devlin2019bert} underpin both the dense sentence embeddings that make related requests easier to retrieve \cite{reimers2019sentencebert} and the compact distilled encoders, such as MiniLM, that keep that step affordable \cite{wang2020minilm}. Approximate nearest-neighbor indexes such as HNSW \cite{malkov2018hnsw}, product quantization \cite{jegou2011pq}, and GPU-accelerated libraries \cite{johnson2019faiss} can search large vector collections, but none of them decides whether a recorded answer satisfies the later request. That decision is closer to natural-language inference, whose benchmark corpora \cite{bowman2015snli} and strong encoders \cite{he2021deberta} VeriGate reuses for its answer-assertion check. Verification can act on the two questions, on a stored answer and incoming task, or on a newly generated response; these evaluate different objects and incur different latency and provider cost.

Cost-aware LLM serving is an active area in its own right: cascades that escalate only hard queries to an expensive model \cite{chen2023frugalgpt} and learned request routers \cite{ong2024routellm} both trade quality against spend, and recent surveys catalogue the broader efficient-inference design space \cite{zhu2023survey}. Caching is complementary to these, avoiding the provider call entirely when a suitable answer already exists.

GPTCache provides configurable embedding, storage, similarity, and post-processing components rather than a single mandatory fixed threshold \cite{bang2023gptcache,gptcache_docs}. vCache learns cache-entry-specific boundaries online and reports a user-specified error-rate bound under its paper's modelling and feedback assumptions \cite{schroeder2026vcache}. Its published benchmarks and guarantee are not reproduced by VeriGate. Krites combines static and dynamic tiers; a near miss can trigger an asynchronous LLM judge and later promotion without putting that judge on the current request's critical path \cite{krites2026}. This differs from VeriGate's synchronous, optional candidate assessment. These systems make it inaccurate to claim that existing caches uniformly trust similarity alone.

\section{Position of This Work}
VeriGate's engineering contribution is the explicit authorized scope, provider-profile compatibility, final candidate identity recheck, and an answer-aware selection path with visible abstentions. Its research contribution is a bounded comparison of baseline and experimental policies on labelled technical tasks and real retrieval paths. It does not introduce semantic caching, adaptive thresholds, or verification as general ideas. A zero-error count on a small selected set is descriptive and is not a formal risk guarantee. Query-pair labels test paraphrase decisions; answer-level labels test whether one stored answer fits a new request. Chapter~6 keeps those denominators separate.
